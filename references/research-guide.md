# Research any university

Use a discovery workflow rather than a database of supported schools. Public-source access varies; a private
portal or an unpublished term requires student-provided documents or sign-in. Never promise universal coverage.

## Discover the right sources

1. Resolve the institution, country, campus, degree level, major, concentration, and mode. Similar school names
   and programs can lead to different rules. Confirm abbreviations rather than guessing.
2. Find the university's official site and its program catalog. Some institutions use contracted catalog or
   registration services: follow links from the official university site to establish their authority.
3. Locate the correct catalog-year program requirements and official degree-audit/academic regulations guidance.
   A current promotional program page may not describe an older student's requirements.
4. Locate the current course descriptions and enrollment conditions. Check for published prerequisite changes.
5. Find the target term's actual schedule: campus, delivery mode, sections, instructor, linked components, and
   applicable session dates. The academic calendar establishes the university's term dates and deadlines.
6. If the result is a PDF, read the appropriate year and program; use page references. If a dynamic page cannot
   be read, try Cubbie's browser, then ask the student for a relevant export. Avoid repeatedly retrying a blocked site.

Useful search patterns, adapted to the local language and terminology:
- "<university> <program> <catalog year> degree requirements"
- "site:<verified official domain> <course code> prerequisites"
- "<university> <target term and year> schedule of classes"
- "<university> <course code> <target term and year> sections"
- "<university> transfer credit equivalency <source institution>"

Do not restrict discovery to .edu sites: institutions worldwide use other domains, module handbooks,
study regulations, and quarter/trimester systems. Do not put a transcript or student identifier in a search.

## Keep claims separate

| Question | Appropriate evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Does the course exist? | Official course description |
| Does it count toward this degree? | Applicable program requirements, approved elective list, or degree audit |
| Is this student eligible? | Current enrollment conditions plus their completion/grade/permission evidence |
| Is it offered this term? | Official target-term schedule for the right campus/mode |
| Who teaches this section? | Official section record, not a professor's historical course list |
| Is there a seat? | Live section record with a timestamp; availability can change |
| Does it fit the career? | Published topics plus an explicitly labeled recommendation |
| Is transfer credit accepted? | Official articulation or the student's approved audit, not title similarity |

An authoritative source for one column is not evidence for the others. Mark unavailable fields unconfirmed.
If search results show a course but the page cannot be opened, treat it as a lead, not verified evidence.

## Prerequisite and schedule reasoning

- Retain the original AND/OR grouping. A requirement of (A OR B) AND C is not satisfied by A alone.
- Distinguish prerequisites, corequisites, recommended preparation, standing, major restrictions, and consent.
- Check minimum grades only when needed. Do not assume "completed" means the minimum grade was earned.
- Record in-progress work separately; passing in the future creates a conditional recommendation.
- Do not assume a transfer course is equivalent, or that an advisor will grant an override.
- A course can satisfy a choice group without being mandatory individually. Do not label every elective required.
- Check lecture, lab, and discussion together, session start/end dates, exam information when published, and
  synchronous online times. An async label does not prove that there are no scheduled assessments.
- Sum the institution's stated units; respect repeat-credit and duplicate-credit rules when published.
- For a future pathway, check the full prerequisite chain for proposed courses. Stop and flag missing or
  circular information rather than inventing a path. Do not promise future term offerings.

## Source quality and stale information

Prefer applicable official regulations and catalog requirements for degree claims; prefer the registrar's
target-term records for sections. A program's archived requirements and current registration rules may both
apply to different questions. When official sources conflict, record both and ask for institutional confirmation.
Do not convert a conflict into a false certainty or silently replace an older student's degree requirements.

Record the page title, direct URL, catalog year or term, date checked, and short paraphrase of the relevant rule.
Link to the relevant course anchor or PDF page where available. Do not manufacture URL fragments.
Reopen source links when preparing a new term plan instead of reusing a cached claim about offerings or seats.

## Working without complete access

Public information should be sufficient for a first career shortlist at many schools. If the official schedule
is unavailable, produce a catalog-based candidate list with offering and instructor fields marked unconfirmed.
If degree rules are unavailable, explain career fit without asserting graduation applicability. Ask for the
single most useful missing document. Keep the research useful without presenting guesses as verified advice.
